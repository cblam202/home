About

I am an Assistant Professor in the Department of Curriculum and Instruction, College of Education, Kansas State University (KSU/K-State).

I am also an accreditation reviewer for the Council for the Accreditation of Educator Preparation (CAEP) and a program reviewer for the Kansas State Department of Education (KSDE).

I also serve as an editorial board member for the Journal of Research in Rural Education (JRRE) and as an assistant editor for the Online Journal of Rural Research and Policy (OJRRP).

I am originally from Phu Yen, Vietnam. I earned a bachelor's degree in Mathematics Education from Ho Chi Minh City University of Education, and master's and doctoral degrees in Curriculum and Instruction from K-State.

My work focuses on sustaining the educator workforce and improving conditions that support educators and school systems.

I also enjoy traveling and learning about the histories and cultures of the places I visit. One of my favorite experiences was visiting Stonehenge in summer 2023.

Stonehenge, summer 2023 - UNESCO World Heritage Site and iconic prehistoric monument.

Other Research Output

In addition to publications and presentations, I contribute to the US Teacher Shortages project, a publicly available dataset maintained with Dr. Tuan Nguyen (University of Missouri) and other colleagues. The project systematically documents state-level teacher vacancies, underqualification, preparation, and turnover across the U.S. It is designed to provide policymakers, researchers, and the public with timely information about staffing challenges across the nation's teacher workforce.

Teaching

I teach undergraduate and graduate courses in data analysis and quantitative research methods, with an emphasis on applications in educational settings.

  • EDCI 514 - Leveraging Data in Educational Settings (3 credits)
    This course offers foundational understandings of data in education, introducing contemporary concepts such as big data and the emerging field of educational data. Topics include recent applications of big data, four major types of educational data (input, outcome, process, and satisfaction), common pitfalls in data interpretation, and strategies for ethical and effective data use in educational settings.
  • EDCI 913 - Inquiry I: Statistical Analysis in Curriculum and Instruction (3 credits)
    This course provides an introduction to statistics and statistical analysis in education. Topics include basic statistical concepts, probability distributions, hypothesis testing, correlation, and simple regression. The course focuses on helping students understand these foundational concepts and procedures, thus enabling them to critically interpret data and statistical results in educational contexts.
  • EDCI 917 - Inquiry IV: Advanced Quantitative Methods in Curriculum and Instruction (3 credits)
    This advanced course in quantitative analysis equips students with the knowledge, skills, and dispositions needed to use quantitative data and statistical analysis responsibly for educational research and decision-making. Topics include scalable data management, data curation, regression analysis, and data visualization, with an emphasis on ethically interpreting and effectively communicating research findings to inform policy and practice. The course is designed for students who intend to apply quantitative methods in their research and professional work.
